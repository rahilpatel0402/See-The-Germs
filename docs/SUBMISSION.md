# Submission text (final, Wed Nov 4)

Order follows the judging weights: impact, Meta tooling, technical depth, demo. Clinician voice first.

## One-liner
Makes invisible contamination chains visible on staged video of care tasks, using SAM 3.1 to track hands and surfaces and Muse Spark to coach the caregiver, so nursing home staff can see which missed hand-hygiene moment spread the pathogen and replay the fix.

## Impact
CDC estimates that 1 to 3 million serious infections occur in US long-term care settings every year ([CDC, Protecting Long-Term Care Residents from Sepsis](https://www.cdc.gov/patient-safety/stories/protecting-ltc-residents-from-sepsis.html)); the estimate still cited for deaths, built in 2000 from small studies of the 1980s and 1990s, put the toll near 388,000 a year ([Strausbaugh and Joseph, Infect Control Hosp Epidemiol 2000;21:674-679, PMID 11083186](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11083186/)), and a 2011 review counted about 2 million infections a year in US nursing homes ([Montoya and Mody, Aging Health 2011, PMC3526889](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3526889/)). Hand hygiene is the primary measure against these infections, in WHO's words ([WHO Guidelines on Hand Hygiene in Health Care, 2009](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241597906)). Staff know the WHO Five Moments. They still miss them, because the thing they are supposed to interrupt is invisible.

I run clinical informatics across more than forty skilled nursing facilities. The tools we have today watch the sanitizer dispenser or rely on a person with a clipboard. Neither shows a certified nursing assistant that the phone she picked up after adjusting a bed rail is now carrying C. auris to the next room. See the Germs shows exactly that, on her own staged clip, in five minutes, with one phone camera and no sensor install.

Who uses it: the infection preventionist who owns the hand hygiene program, and the CNA who gets coached. The loop is staged clip, glow, one missed moment, one counterfactual, one coaching note.

It is a staff training and infection-prevention audit aid on staged, synthetic footage. It does not diagnose, treat, or advise patients, and gives no clinical recommendation about any person. It is not surveillance.

## Use of Meta AI tooling
- SAM 3.1 on the Meta Model API is the only segmentation and tracking layer, used as a contact and transmission sensor, which is not what it was built for. One short noun phrase per class, one call per class per clip, clips uploaded once through the Files API, every mask track cached. "Bare hand" and "gloved hand" are separate classes, which is how glove on and glove off are detected with no manual markers.
- Scene setup is a loop between the two models: Muse Spark vision looks at three frames and proposes every prop with its zone, its role, and phrases that describe appearance the way SAM matches; SAM tests each phrase on a frame through the image endpoint; anything unfound goes back to Spark for plainer phrases. Spark proposes, SAM verifies, for a few cents.
- SAM box prompts on video: a prop neither model could name is boxed once on a frame and SAM tracks it forward and backward through the clip, from the UI.
- Muse Spark 1.3 vision adjudicates only the contact candidates the engine flags as ambiguous, including contacts on an object the camera could not see at that moment: two annotated frames in, one of three verdicts and one sentence out, structured output at temperature 0, cached and capped per clip, with touches of a resident or a dispenser, long holds, and stacked props always reviewed outside the cap. Every vote is marked on the timeline with the frames it saw. SAM finds, Spark adjudicates, the engine decides.
- Muse Spark 1.3 text writes the coaching note and the audit summary from the structured record only, validated against a schema, word counts, and a clinical-language filter, and never changes the analysis.
- The same two Spark roles run end to end on Muse Spark Open through Fireworks or Baseten partner inference, selected by one environment variable, with outputs compared against the Meta Model API in the README: the open-weight model agreed with the hosted one on 14 of 15 adjudications through Fireworks and 12 of 14 through Baseten across the demo clips, and the narratives reached the same top fix on each (`npm run spark-compare`).

## Technical depth
- A pure, deterministic TypeScript engine: masks, contacts with a confident and an ambiguous band, hygiene with pathogen-specific effectiveness, glove on and off from SAM object births, propagation as a state machine over a merged event timeline, Five Moments mapping, exposure score, counterfactual as a rerun, hands assigned per caregiver when more than one person is in frame (opt in, measured on one outside clip). One hundred forty node:test cases, including the clip A chain and the MRSA versus C. difficile rub.
- Hardened on footage it had never seen: nine public hand-hygiene and skills-lab training videos ran through the live path on a local machine and were checked frame by frame (none is in the demo). That round found that SAM follows a hand through a glove change, which the glove detector now handles; added vision review for any touch that lasts a fifth of the clip, because a prop between the camera and the hands reads as a long hold; detects shot changes and never carries a prop across one; and raised Moment 1 at the first approach of a clip.
- The same clip, adjudications, scenario, and pathogen always produce the same output. The counterfactual slider is instant because of it.
- Accuracy is measured, not asserted: contacts are hand-labeled from frame sheets before looking at the engine's output and `npm run accuracy` reports precision and recall at one second with every miss and false alarm named. Measured on four staged clips: A 1.00 precision and 1.00 recall, B 0.67 and 1.00, C 0.86 and 1.00, feasibility 0.86 and 1.00. Every false alarm is named in the README, and every one is a depth ambiguity the camera cannot resolve: a squeeze beside the resident, an open palm held above the rail, or a touch registering early as the hand approached.
- Limitations are stated in the README: 2D contact heuristic mitigated by staging, dwell, and adjudication; glove detection measured on staged clips and nine public training clips; Moment 3 out of scope; heuristic score weights.
